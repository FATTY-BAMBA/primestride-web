# StrideBrain demo content and disclosure standard

## Customer-facing voice
Present StrideBrain as a usable operations product. Prefer "sample workspace", "demo data", "example projects", and "interactive demo" over repeated "fictional", "synthetic", "simulation", and "restricted private preview" copy. Lead with business outcomes and product capability. Use "access code" rather than technical security terminology on visitor-facing invitation screens.

## Accuracy boundary
Maintain a visible, discreet "Demo data" label on pages showing sample company information. When a user might reasonably interpret an action as real (approving a quote, sending a customer response, creating a supplier order, importing historical costs), explicitly say what happened and whether any external delivery or database write occurred. Keep source provenance and public-reference vs. sample-data distinctions intact.

Do not claim:
- sample activity was a real customer or completed company engagement;
- AI has read genuine company data, when it has only consulted example records;
- an unsent quote, email, or approval was transmitted;
- a provider is connected when its live request failed.

## Apply consistently
Guest onboarding, navigation/status labels, conversational Copilot answers, tool descriptions, sample-document explanations, result/source cards, quotations and approval outcomes, and bilingual English / Traditional Chinese strings. Production runbooks and internal security documentation should retain precise "synthetic", "internal", "fictional" terminology where technically necessary.

## Quality gate
At review, scan customer-facing strings for gratuitous caveats and jargon. Keep important operational disclosures readable, not hidden. This content refinement is separate from technical security work; guardrails and access restrictions are not relaxed.
